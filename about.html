03 Heritage

An older lineage.

The lineage of the maison is older than haircare. It belongs to the apothecary and the parfumeur — houses that composed slowly, that signed for their work, that understood scent as memory and formula as discipline.

These are old practices. They have been refined for centuries. They are returned to without apology.

04 The Founder

Masiela Lusha

Founder & Creative Director

The maison is signed by its founder.

Masiela Lusha is the Founder and Creative Director of MAISON D’VUE, the House of Affinity Compositions™.

Origin

She was born in Tirana, Albania, in 1985, and left as a child refugee with her family during the country’s political turbulence. They moved through Budapest and Vienna before settling in Michigan. By the time she arrived, she had already lived inside three languages. English was the fourth. The slowness of becoming fluent — of learning a place by learning its words — is the formative discipline of her working life.

The Writer

She began writing early. Her first book of poetry, Inner Thoughts, was published when she was twelve, in two languages. The book made her, by the Michigan press’s account, the youngest author in the world to publish in two languages simultaneously. She was named among North America’s Top Ten Talented Poets and received recognition from President Bill Clinton before graduating high school at fifteen. She was admitted to UCLA as a junior at eighteen and graduated with a degree in English.

Five books of poetry followed Inner Thoughts: Drinking the Moon, Amore Celeste, The Call, and The Living Air. A novel, The Besa, written in a single month and named for the ancient Albanian code of honor and hospitality that predates her nation. Two children’s books, Boopity Boop! Writes Her First Poem and Boopity Boop! Goes To Hawaii, written in three languages. And, in a labor she has called the most precious of her career, translations of Mother Teresa’s poems and prayers from their original Albanian.

Her most recent work, The 12 Veils of Hope, is a sequence of one hundred and thirty-seven haiku arranged in twelve movements — a meditation on the passage of light and loss, composed in the most withholding form the language offers. The book became an Amazon best seller in modern poetry and in female writers. Its architecture — movements, not chapters — would later inform the structure of MAISON D’VUE itself.

A poet composes by selection — the right word in the right line, the right silence between them. A maison composes the same way.

The Actress

Audiences recognize Masiela as an actress. She made her television debut at fifteen and went on to portray Carmen Lopez in the Warner Bros. Television series George Lopez, which aired on ABC for five seasons and was globally syndicated across more than seventy countries. The role earned her two consecutive Young Artist Awards. She has appeared in over thirty film and television projects, including Sony Pictures’ Blood: The Last Vampire and SyFy’s Sharknado franchise. She has lent her voice, in Clifford’s Puppy Days on PBS, to a generation of children who learned to read alongside her.

The Humanitarian

Her humanitarian work has been continuous since her early twenties. She has served as ambassador to Prince Harry’s Sentebale, the World Assembly of Youth, and Athgo International. She founded her own organization, Children of the World. She is an active advocate for UN Women.

The Maison

MAISON D’VUE is, in her telling, a seventh book — a composition. A poet selects the right word and places it in the right line. A maison selects the right botanical and places it in the right composition. The two disciplines are, she believes, the same discipline.

“If a word in poetry can be replaced or entirely removed, it should. I live by the same discipline in formulating.”

Fourteen rare essences, signed in a single scent, made in the patience that the work requires.

She lives in Los Angeles with her husband, two children, and their dog, Hemingway.
